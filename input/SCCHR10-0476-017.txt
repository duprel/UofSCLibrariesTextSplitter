RECOMMENDATIONS
adopted at
The Annual Meeting
Columbia, S.C.
April 17, 1947

RE: SOUTH CAROLINA DIVISION ORGANIZATION

I. In order to broaden the scope of our work, and to develop
sounder, more effective programs, it is recommended that
four standing committees be appointed to set up goals for
study and action, to communicate these to the members, and to
organizations with similar interests, and to work with them
in order to gain sufficient strength to make our combined
efforts produce results. These four standing committees on
programs are as follows:

1. An Education Committee
2. A Health Committee
3. suffrage
A Recreation Committee
4. A Law Enforcement Committee

II. In order to implement the work of the program committees, it
is recommended that the following committees be set up, some
of the duties of which are given below:

1. A Publicity Committee to conduct an intensive and continu-
ous program of publicity through the radio, press, and
through public relations with individuals and groups;
this publicity to include interpretation as well as
announcements and reports of meetings, activities, etc.

2. A Membership Committee to launch at the earliest date
possible and practicable a membership drive to build a
large and representative membership committed to the
broad and democratic programs of the Southern Regional
Council, and especially to the program emphases of the
South Carolina Division, and to work continuously to build
a mass membership, strong enough to make its influence
felt.

3. A Finance Committee to work out an annual budget, solicit
funds from members, interested individuals and organiza-
tions, and corporations, and to explore sources of further
income so as to put the program on a sound financial basis,
and to make needed expansion possible.

III. In order to extend the influence and effectiveness of the
South Carolina Division, Southern Regional Council, it is rec-
ommended that an Advisory Council be set up to function as
suggested below, and in other appropriate ways; to work with
the Executive Board, independently, and through its own
committees as listed below.

1. The Advisory Council to help design specific programs of
study and action, to call upon state departments of educa-
tion, health, welfare, labor, et al., and state colleges
and the university to supply facts, speakers and discussion
leaders, and to help train lay leaders both for the South
Carolina Division and other lay groups.

2. A Student Committee to develop cooperatively a program
with students.

3. A Committee on Christian Social Relations to develop cooper-
atively a program with denominational, interdenominational,
women's, and young peoples groups, with groups of the
Catholic and Jewish faiths, and with the Y. M. C. A.,
Y. W. C. A., Salvation Army, etc.

4. A Citizens Committee to develop cooperatively programs
with such organizations as men's service clubs, women's
clubs, patriotic organizations, et al.
